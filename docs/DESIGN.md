# Scope and source decisions

The design basis is the user's two **Math Tower Evolution** documents, followed by their explicit confirmation of 1,000 Towers plus 999 Interludes and their later instruction to implement **only the first Tower** for now.

## Current delivery

The complete first Tower now runs in 3D at `midnight/`. The user's latest direction supersedes the playful animated-feature look: a serious, grounded research-institute mystery with a rebuilt introduction, human-scale characters, worn materials and restrained lighting. All 21 floors, the nine required and three optional mathematics systems, story scenes, choices, transport and return-to-lobby escape are implemented. See [the current 3D scope](TOWER-1-3D.md). The earlier 2D adaptation remains available at the root.

One playable Tower definition, one shared Tower engine, shared browser systems, replaceable numeric math banks, sequential checked help, local saves and static hosting support. There are no later Tower definitions and no implemented Interlude engines or stages.

The stage sequence reserves Tower 1 → Interlude 1 → Tower 2 → … → Interlude 999 → Tower 1000. Reserving IDs does not make those adventures playable.

## Tower 1 interpretation

- Eighteen above-ground floors plus B1–B3. The goal is to reach the B3 override and return to the lobby, not finish at B3.
- The twelve source math sockets include optional supplies, a side classroom and an emergency battery. This implementation counts **nine required and three optional** panels.
- Public Lift A serves 18–10. Research Lift B serves 16, 14, 12, 10, 8, 6, 4, 2 and B1, after power restoration. The 3D layout now uses two continuous enclosed fire stairs and fixed lift shafts; the decorative escalators are removed. Freight Lift C serves 8, 4, 1 and B2 after the optional power allocation. B3 has an explicit service-stair connection to B2; the freight lift does not invent an unlisted B3 stop.
- The optional maintenance shortcut consistently connects Floor 8 to Floor 6.
- Lighting remains legible regardless of optional supplies. Power allocation can enable freight or clear lower patrols through ventilation; the mutually exclusive allocations cannot accumulate beyond the ten-unit budget.
- Action pauses at every formal math panel. The document's escalating danger after wrong answers and wrong-answer bypass are not implemented. Students can leave and return, but required mathematical work still gates completion.
- The battery gives escape protection rather than bypassing a required math assessment.
- Guidebot and researcher choices persist. The researcher follows visually; this is not independent escort AI. Human-proportioned shadowed figures replace spiders and hostile Guidebots in the 3D game. Directed pistol fire and a short frontal shove create escape opportunities. Enemies do not navigate the transport graph themselves.
- Q.E.D. is museum lore only in Tower 1. Its later rescue and the next adventures are deferred.

## Math and story remain separate

Formal questions come from the selected bank. Gameplay choices such as transportation and power allocation are world decisions. The dimensional fiction is not presented as established mathematical or scientific fact.

Foundations uses rotating seeded examples corresponding to the source's arithmetic topics. Readiness demonstrates swapping in fractions, percentages and numerical equation answers. Neither is a complete test-preparation curriculum. The two conflicting long-range curriculum timelines in the documents are not resolved by inventing later levels here.

The ending stops after Tower 1. Campus Sprint has a design script but is deliberately not implemented under the latest scope instruction.
