# Math Tower — a new visual identity

**Historical selection: C — The Midnight Museum. The latest user direction supersedes its playful style with a serious, grounded mystery; see [the current implementation](TOWER-1-3D.md).** The repository includes the [selected visual target](concepts/midnight-museum-target.png), [ten-panel storyboard](concepts/tower-1-storyboard.png), and [100-direction catalog](visual-directions.tsv). The separate offline visual-study gallery contains all six rendered finalists. See [the playable opening scope](MIDNIGHT-OPENING.md) for what has actually been implemented.

This study responds to the user's rejection of the current implementation and their clarification: **cinematic story scenes plus playable arcade action**. The current game's visual layout is not a starting point for the redesign.

The package contains **100 conceptual visual directions**, organized around 100 distinct arcade/action/adventure game references, **six illustrated finalists**, and a cinematic treatment for the whole of Tower 1. It does not contain 100 rendered screenshots. Reference sites could not be reached through this environment's network proxy; the reference analysis draws on established game-art knowledge, not a newly verified screenshot survey. The illustrations are newly generated original concept images, not existing-game screenshots or finished gameplay assets.

## The change that matters

The first implementation made the building a collection of rows, and the player a small token moving between panels. A new palette would leave that problem intact.

The new visual unit is **a place the player inhabits**: a storm-lashed roof, an enormous kinetic exhibit, a malfunctioning robotics demonstration, a cramped elevator, an impossible laboratory. The camera follows the character's experience. Architecture, animation, acting, sound and physical consequences carry the story.

The visual rules are:

- No stacked-floor building cutaway, repeated corridor strips or spreadsheet-like overview as the main play screen.
- A clearly readable protagonist with an expressive face, body language and a distinctive silhouette. The teal jacket and mustard backpack in these studies are provisional casting and costume ideas, not a settled protagonist identity.
- Rooms vary in shape, height, composition, depth and purpose. A gallery and a basement apparatus must not feel like differently colored versions of the same rectangle.
- Foreground, action space and background have separate visual roles. Spectacle must leave an unmistakable playable route.
- Hazards announce themselves through motion and sound. Combat has anticipation, contact, recovery and visible consequence.
- Story events are staged and animated. Exposition appears through people and things doing something, supported by voice, subtitles and sound.
- Formal mathematics belongs to a visible object in the scene: a lock, control console, instrument or geometric mechanism. Camera and typography make it readable without returning to a dashboard.
- The 1,000 Towers and 999 Interludes remain the long-term design scope. Only Tower 1 is presently authorized for implementation.

## The six finalists

### A — Lantern & Shadow

**A luminous painted adventure.** Warm pools of light, deep blue impossible space, expressive hand-drawn motion and enormous sculptural exhibits. The player seems to have stepped into a moving painting.

The camera travels diagonally through a room, pulls wide for a reveal and comes close for acting. A jump traces a strong sweeping arc across the composition. Layers of painted foreground and background create depth, while the playable surface remains simpler and clearer.

Math activates the artwork: a correct calibration steadies an orrery, changes a beam of light or unfolds a bridge. The number entry remains an actual readable field associated with the instrument.

Useful reference principles: the fluid silhouettes of *Ori*, the painted vitality of *Rayman Legends*, the luminous physical sets of *Trine*. The characters and museum are original.

**Strongest quality:** wonder and emotional atmosphere. **Production demand:** substantial environment painting and character animation. A plausible browser route is layered 2.5D staging, not a single flat background image.

### B — Inkstorm

**A graphic novel with the energy of an arcade action film.** Strong contour lines, controlled halftone, two-tone shadows, turquoise and vermilion accents, aggressive diagonal staging and expressive impact poses.

The camera has a clear three-quarter action view that can move into cinematic close-ups. A sweeping exhibit arm creates a readable dodge; a leap has a brief stretched pose; a stun has a crisp graphic impact and a satisfying recovery animation. This is not a page of comic panels or a row-based brawler.

Math is part of the graphic language: a clean ivory answer field appears on a physical machine, then the frame visibly changes when its mechanism works. Effects become quiet while the learner thinks.

Useful reference principles: *Hades*' silhouette hierarchy, *Hi-Fi Rush*'s animated acting, *Viewtiful Joe*'s impact staging. No rhythm test is imposed on mathematics.

**Strongest quality:** immediate arcade character and control readability. **Production demand:** a cohesive animation library, disciplined graphic backgrounds and carefully limited camera effects. This is the strongest alternative if action energy takes priority over a fully sculpted 3D film look.

### C — The Midnight Museum

**An original animated feature that becomes playable.** Appealing stylized 3D characters, tactile materials, oversized mechanical exhibits, facial acting, theatrical lighting and a camera that moves naturally between film and action.

The opening could establish a character through a glance, a small nervous gesture and a brave decision. A damaged Guidebot is funny and unsettling because of how it moves and hesitates. An enormous pendulum is an actual moving obstacle, not scenery behind a quiz.

The camera normally shows one readable action space and the hero at a useful scale. Story shots use the same characters, objects and sets. As a shot ends, the camera settles into the control angle and the player continues the movement.

Useful reference principles: *Luigi's Mansion 3*'s expressive physical comedy, *Ratchet & Clank*'s continuous cinematic staging, *Psychonauts 2*'s original caricature and strange architecture.

**Strongest quality:** the clearest match for “a movie plus a real game.” **Production demand:** highest sustained asset burden: models, rigs, facial animation, environmental interaction and browser performance work. The concept image is a visual target, not evidence that a browser implementation already reaches that quality.

### D — Paper Paradox

**A sophisticated stop-motion world made of geometry.** Folded card, tracing paper, brass fasteners, visible edges, layered shadows and expressive paper figures. An impossible room can literally unfold around the player.

The camera explores a single crafted set. Paper bridges curl, stairs fan open, geometric creatures fold into new poses, and a museum exhibit tears to reveal another space behind it. A bridge changing form after a calculation becomes a physical event with a beginning, middle and end.

Useful reference principles: the material honesty of *Tearaway*, the physical connections of *LittleBigPlanet*, the transformation language of *Paper Mario*. It must feel like a premium adventurous animated film rather than an elementary-school craft activity.

**Strongest quality:** mathematics and visual identity naturally reinforce one another. **Production demand:** carefully authored folding and character animation; repeated “paper unfolds” effects would quickly become a gimmick without varied mechanics.

### E — The Golden Impossible

**A supernatural Art Deco mathematical institution.** Malachite, black lacquer, ribbed brass, amber glass, sunburst mechanisms and impossible spatial fractures. It is elegant, eccentric and recognizably its own place.

A grand exhibit rotates out of alignment while the camera holds a beautiful, clear composition. A quiet scientific institution becomes impossible in a very specific way. The grandeur is architectural rather than dependent on filling the screen with enemies.

Useful reference principles: monumental designed institutions, the moving architecture of *Dishonored 2*, and the spatial clarity of sculptural puzzle worlds. Mature themes and violence from any reference do not transfer into this child-facing game.

**Strongest quality:** a durable world identity capable of carrying many different adventures. **Production demand:** strong environment design, material consistency and expressive characters to prevent the result feeling cold or decorative. This architectural direction could also support C's animation language.

### F — Prism Runner

**Modern isometric pixel cinema.** Carefully composed color clusters, expressive readable sprites, rich diagonal spaces and spectacular geometric light. It is deliberately different from an old side-view elevator game.

Action flows across one three-dimensional-looking chamber. The camera moves closer for pixel animation sequences, rather than leaving the protagonist as a tiny token. A prism changes the route and light through the room when a mathematical instrument is restored.

Useful reference principles: *CrossCode*'s action clarity, *Hyper Light Drifter*'s color hierarchy, and *Sea of Stars*' environmental light.

**Strongest quality:** excellent arcade readability and a distinctive achievable visual vocabulary. **Production demand:** pixel animation is still substantial craft. **Fit caution:** this is the least direct match for the user's request for an animated-movie experience, so it is a deliberate alternative rather than the leading recommendation.

## Comparison

These judgments are art-direction assessments, not measured performance benchmarks.

| Direction | Film-like character acting | Arcade clarity | Signature strength | Main production burden |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A: Lantern & Shadow | Strong | Strong with restrained effects | Painted wonder | Art and frame animation |
| B: Inkstorm | Strong | Very strong | Graphic action | Poses, timing and consistent visual rules |
| C: Midnight Museum | Very strong | Strong with a controlled camera | Playable animated film | 3D assets, rigs and animation |
| D: Paper Paradox | Strong and distinctive | Strong with clean silhouettes | Geometry as physical material | Folding and stop-motion-style animation |
| E: Golden Impossible | Strong | Strong with clear floor planes | Original architectural identity | Set design and material consistency |
| F: Prism Runner | More stylized and limited | Very strong | Modern pixel action | Pixel art and sprite animation |

**Recommendation: C for the stated movie ambition; B as the strongest action-first alternative.** A is the strongest painterly alternative. D is the most conceptually unusual. E offers a world identity that could support C, while F remains a deliberate pixel-art choice.

## From a look to a real game

The selected direction needs an animation and interaction proof before another full implementation. A suitable first proof is the opening on the roof and the first museum encounter: cinematic arrival, a control handoff, movement with weight, a meaningful dodge or jump, one animated environmental consequence, and one typed mathematical instrument.

The proof must demonstrate moving characters, camera choreography, sound, physical interaction and readable mathematical input in the chosen visual style. A beautiful still image with controls layered over it would not validate the redesign.

Only after that proof should the whole first Tower be rebuilt. There is no plan here to generate the other 999 Towers or the 999 Interludes prematurely.
